## 0. Headline conclusion

**Observed.** With both knobs at their shipping defaults, `notice` on the heavy
band is **0.523 / 0.555 / 0.596 ms** across three like-for-like runs (mean 0.558,
spread 0.073), and on the exact heavy-B anchor **0.520 / 0.550 / 0.600 / 0.642 ms**
across four (mean 0.578, spread 0.122) — reproducing the 0.56 ms prior. Delaying the doorbell by a
measured `pub` of up to **≈0.33 ms is free**: `notice` falls roughly 1:1 with the
delay over that range and `period` does not rise above its unmodified run-to-run
band. Past that point `notice` stops falling — it flattens at **0.237 ms** — and
the inserted delay starts landing in the frame. The post-knee slope is
**measured, not assumed**: regressing `notice + pub` on `pub` over the four arms
past the knee gives **0.971** (0.917 with the membership-anomalous D=300 arm
dropped, 0.990 per-window) against the 1.000 the model predicts — see §3, which
also explains why the *chord* across the whole sweep reads ≈0.65 and why that is
the expected signature of a knee, not a contradiction of it. A piecewise fit
`notice(pub) = floor + max(0, K − pub)` over the seven arms (118–149 blocked
frames each) gives **K = 0.335 ms, floor = 0.237 ms**, bootstrap 90 % CI
**K ∈ [0.275, 0.390]** on window noise alone, widening to **[0.260, 0.410]** once
the measured run-to-run spread is folded in; **floor ∈ [0.208, 0.266]**. That fit
beats a pure-linear alternative 10.9× on SSE (0.0029 vs 0.0320) and the no-effect
null 35× (0.1041). Separately, coarsening the host's `C_DONE` poll never lowered
`notice`: at `poll_us` = 0 / 50 / 250 / 1000 µs it reads 0.595 / 0.443 / 0.531 /
**0.744** ms.

All of the above is on the brief's window filter — heavy band, `blocked ≥ 90 %`.
The looser `blocked ≥ 60 %` set is carried throughout as a sensitivity and moves
the knee down to 0.303 ms; §1b states the filter choice explicitly and §3 prints
both fits side by side, because the difference straddles the Stage C decision
bar.

**Inferred.** Roughly **0.335 ms — 60 % of the 0.558 ms `notice` — is fabric-side
dead time at the doorbell.** A delay the fabric absorbs for free can only be free
because the fabric was not going to act on the doorbell during it, which is the
`S_SNAP_*` window by construction. That measured dead window is ~2.1× the
**0.158 ms** `S_SNAP` design figure carried in from prior work, so `S_SNAP` (or
whatever else holds the FSM off `C_SUBMIT`) runs materially longer than designed.
**The remaining 0.237 ms floor is NOT attributed by this sweep** — it is
`C_DONE`-write-to-host-visible plus the fabric's own submit-detect latency once
it *is* listening, and the doorbell probe cannot separate those two. What the
sweep does settle about the host side is narrower and negative: **the shipping
pure spin is already at the host-side floor**; poll strategy is a lever that only
points the wrong way.

**The decision-relevant number for Stage C:** the *proven* fabric-side share is
**0.335 ms**, and the bootstrap puts **P(K ≥ 0.35) at 0.32** counting window
noise only, **0.35** with the run-to-run spread folded in — call it **roughly one
chance in three**, on a 7-point fit from a single session. Spec §5 R1's threshold
is "no fabric fix sized ≥ 0.35 ms". The point estimate is **0.015 ms short of
that bar and the bar is inside the CI.** A Stage C fix that closes only the
doorbell dead window is therefore **more likely than not to fall short on its
own** — it would need part of the 0.237 ms floor as well, or R1's LW-bridge
branch — but a ~1-in-3 chance of clearing the bar outright is not a basis for
ruling the fix out. (On the looser `blocked ≥ 60 %` set the same probability is
0.08; the filter choice moves this number across the decision bar, which is why
§1b and §3 make it explicit.)

### 1a. Four deviations from the task brief, and why

1. **`--secs 55`, not 30.** Heavy-B is not reachable in 30 s. It occurs at frame
   index ≈1890–2010, **≈31.5–33.5 s of engine wall-clock into the run**, and the
   Task-3 30 s run stopped at `n=1770`. 55 s reaches `n≈3450–3480` on every arm.
2. **A fourth poll arm, `poll_us=1000`, was added.** `poll_us=250` should
   mechanically add ~125 µs of observation granularity to `notice` and did not,
   which would have left the Step 5 null uninterpretable — indistinguishable from
   "the knob does nothing". §4 shows the knob does work.
3. **A fourth unmodified replicate was run last** (run 13), so the late poll arms
   would have an unmodified neighbour rather than only early ones. It came back
   anomalous in duration — see Table 3 † and §7.7 — and is used only on the exact
   anchor.
4. **Arms are matched on `cov_px`, not on a frame-index plateau.** The brief's
   Step 1 asked for a *plateau* every arm could be read on. **There is no
   plateau** (§1b): heavy-B is a short transient, and the frame index at which it
   occurs drifts between arms. Matching on `cov_px` value is what replaces it.
   The `blocked ≥ 90 %` restriction the brief's Step 3 specified is kept as the
   primary filter; `blocked ≥ 60 %` is reported alongside as a sensitivity, not
   substituted for it.

### 1b. Scene identity

Confirmed **numerically**, never by triangle count. `cov_px = 213358` — the
heavy-B anchor — appears in **every one of the thirteen logs**, at
`fabric_ms[frame] = 16.685–16.690 ms`, matching the W2 post-clear heavy-B figure
of 16.68/16.69 ms. That co-occurrence of an exact coverage count with an exact
fabric time, reproduced thirteen times, is the identity evidence.

**Correction carried in from the corpus work: heavy-B is a transient, not a
plateau.** The brief and the W2 write-up both describe heavy-B as a plateau. It
is not, and any later task that tries to capture it as one will miss it.
Measured, in every one of the thirteen logs: `cov_px` sits at ≈151,150–151,946
for several windows, ramps through one window at 176,000–200,900, peaks at
**exactly 213,358 for one or two consecutive 30-frame windows**, and collapses
the next window to 105,000–141,000. Onset to collapse is **at most three
windows — ~90 frames, ~1.5 s** — with the 213,358 peak sampled on **30–60
frames**. It occupies frame index **n ≈ 1890–2010**, i.e. ≈31.5–33.5 s of engine
wall-clock (the answer to the brief's Step 1 "which wall-clock segment"), which
is why a 30 s run cannot reach it.

**Frame-index matching is therefore unusable as the pairing key.** The
`ingame-stage1` `joy_script` is wall-clock driven, so arms of different speed
enter the same game state at different frame indices, and a ~90-frame transient
has no tolerance for that drift. Measured: against the D=0 arm, only **50–75 of
~115 same-`n` windows matched `cov_px` exactly** (43–65 %), with worst-case
deviation **81 %** (D=400 at `n=2310`) late in the run; across two *different
engine builds* (the pre-W2 `20260730-175503` log and today's probe build) the
trajectory is exact for the first 20 windows and first diverges at `n=600`
(36/59 common indices exact). `cov_px` is a stable *fingerprint*; the frame index
at which it occurs is not. **Matching on `cov_px` value is what makes the
cross-arm comparison valid at all**, and it is what §7.3's membership argument
rests on. Two nested sets are used:

- **exact anchor** — `cov_px == 213358` (1–2 windows/arm, `frame` pinned to
  within 0.005 ms across all seven arms);
- **heavy band** — `cov_px ≥ 205000` and **`blocked ≥ 90 %`** (4–5 windows,
  118–149 blocked frames per arm). **This is the primary set**, and
  `blocked ≥ 90 %` is the restriction the task brief's Step 3 specified. The
  looser `blocked ≥ 60 %` variant (5–7 windows, 146–197 blocked frames) is
  reported alongside everywhere the knee is quoted, because it is the only
  analysis choice in this document that moves the headline across the Stage C
  decision bar (K 0.335 → 0.303, `P(K ≥ 0.35)` 0.32 → 0.08). The tighter filter
  is also the better-conditioned one: it fits with **SSE 0.00294 against
  0.00519**, so the extra windows the loose filter admits are adding noise, not
  depth.

**What the "heavy band" actually pools, and why the pooling is legitimate.** It
is *not* one contiguous region. At `blocked ≥ 90 %` each arm's band is drawn from
two or three separated frame regions: the heavy-B transient at `n ≈ 1890–2010`
(`cov_px` 211k–213k), sometimes an intermediate at `n ≈ 2340–2400` (212k–223k),
and an arrival cluster at `n ≈ 2490–2640` (229k–249k). What makes pooling them
valid is not that they are the same scene — they are not — but that **the
membership matches across arms**: every single arm contains the exact anchor
`cov_px = 213,358` and the exact arrival anchor `cov_px = 245,346`, and the
arm-mean `cov_px` spans only 229,565–236,757 (3.1 %). §7.3 shows what residual
mismatch remains and that its direction is conservative for the knee.

Windows are pooled **weighted by blocked-frame count** (`round(blocked% × 30)`),
because `notice` is a per-blocked-frame mean and windows differ in how many
frames blocked. That weighting is applied to every pooled quantity in this
document, `spin_avg` included.

## 3. The knee, and what it attributes

Two poolings of the same windows, same answer. **They are not independent
instruments.** Readout B is `period − frame` on a subset of the windows Readout A
pools, and on a ≈100 %-blocked window `period − frame` *is* `notice + pub` to
within the reconciliation residual in §7.6 — so B is algebraically tied to A.
What agreement between them establishes is that **the knee is not an artefact of
the pooling rule or of the window filter**, which is worth checking because the
two use different window sets and different weightings. It is a consistency
check, not corroboration by a second measurement.

**Readout A — `notice` vs `pub` (primary).** Piecewise fit
`notice(pub) = floor + max(0, K − pub)` over the seven arm means of Table 1
(`blocked ≥ 90 %`), grid-searched on K with `floor` solved in closed form:

| model | SSE | RMS |
|---|---|---|
| **piecewise, K=0.335, floor=0.237** | **0.00294** | 0.0205 |
| linear `0.464 − 0.323·pub` | 0.03196 | 0.0676 |
| flat (no effect) | 0.10411 | — |

The piecewise model beats linear **10.9×** on SSE and the null **35×**.

Bootstrap, 2000 resamples of windows within each arm (seed 20260731; script at
`docs/superpowers/findings/data/2026-07-31-phase4-w3/w3_notice_fit.py`):

| parameter | median | 90 % CI (window noise) | 90 % CI (+ run-to-run) |
|---|---|---|---|
| knee **K** | 0.330 ms | **[0.275, 0.390]** | **[0.260, 0.410]** |
| floor | 0.238 ms | [0.208, 0.266] | [0.190, 0.278] |
| | | **P(K ≥ 0.35) = 0.32** | **P(K ≥ 0.35) = 0.35** |

The first CI resamples windows within arms only. The second additionally applies
an independent `N(0, 0.036)` offset per arm — the measured run-to-run sd from
Table 3's three comparable replicates — because each arm is one run and a
run-level offset is a real, measured source of error the window bootstrap cannot
see. **Quote the probability as ≈0.32–0.35, i.e. roughly one in three**, and not
to two significant figures: it comes from a 7-point fit in a single session.

**Sensitivity to the window filter.** The same procedure on the `blocked ≥ 60 %`
set (Table 1a):

| set | K | floor | SSE | bootstrap median K | 90 % CI (window) | P(K ≥ 0.35) window / +run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`blocked ≥ 90 %` (brief's filter, primary)** | **0.335** | **0.237** | **0.00294** | 0.330 | [0.275, 0.390] | **0.32 / 0.35** |
| `blocked ≥ 60 %` (sensitivity) | 0.303 | 0.236 | 0.00519 | 0.300 | [0.245, 0.355] | 0.08 / 0.15 |

The floor is filter-insensitive (0.236 vs 0.237). The knee is not: 0.032 ms of
the answer, and most of the decision probability, rides on the filter. The
primary set is `blocked ≥ 90 %` because that is what the task brief specified and
because it is the tighter fit; the `≥ 60 %` row is printed so a reader can see
the whole range the corpus supports.

**What the fit predicts at zero delay.** `notice(0) = floor + K = 0.572`. The
D=0 arm (0.555) **is** in the fit, so this is not an out-of-sample check on that
point. What *is* out of sample are the two other unmodified replicates —
Step 1 (0.523) and P=0 (0.596), neither of which enters the fit — and 0.572 sits
inside that pair.

### The slope: why the chord reads ≈0.65 and the post-knee slope reads ≈1.0

This is the single most likely place for a reader to conclude the result is
wrong, so it is stated explicitly. Readout B's own table looks like it rises at
**0.53**, not 1:1 — `(1.090 − 0.605) / (0.910 − 0.000) = 0.533`. Table 1's
`period` column gives the same impression: `(19.843 − 19.240) / 0.967 = 0.623` on
the `≥ 60 %` set.

**Those are chords across the whole sweep, and a chord across a knee must be
shallower than 1.** Under `notice(pub) = floor + max(0, K − pub)` the quantity
that rises 1:1 past the knee is `notice + pub`, and its chord from 0 to X is
`(X − K)/X` — flat below K, unit-slope above, averaged. So a knee at K = 0.335
over X = 0.910 *predicts* a chord of **0.632**, and over X = 0.986 predicts
0.660. Measured chords: **0.669** on `notice + pub` over the primary heavy band
(implying K = 0.327), **0.695** on the `≥ 60 %` set (implying K = 0.295), and
**0.533** on the anchor's `period − frame` (implying K = 0.425, the anchor being
the noisier readout). The shallow chord is the expected signature of the knee,
not evidence against it.

**The local post-knee slope is the one that should be 1.** Regressing
`notice + pub` on `pub` over the four arms with `pub > 0.30`:

| set | post-knee arms | slope | slope, D=300 dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| heavy band, `blocked ≥ 90 %` (arm means) | D=300/400/600/900 | **0.971** | 0.917 |
| heavy band, `blocked ≥ 60 %` (arm means) | D=300/400/600/900 | 0.962 | 0.903 |
| heavy band, `blocked ≥ 90 %` (per-window, blocked-weighted, n=19) | — | 0.990 | 0.945 |

D=300 is the arm §7.3 flags for heavy membership; the result does not depend on
whether it is kept. **Slope ≈0.92–0.99 against a predicted 1.00** — the inserted
delay does land 1:1 in the frame past the knee. The earlier draft of this
document asserted 1:1 without computing it; the number above is the computation.

**The knob demonstrably took effect.** `spin_avg` — the mean `C_DONE` poll

- **At 50 and 250 µs: no, not beyond the noise.** 0.443 and 0.531 against an

- **At 1000 µs: yes, and upward.** 0.744, clearly above the band (+0.19 over the

`≲0.0015 ms` in §3's attribution table and as `≲1.5 µs` in §5 is this same
number. Every coarsening tested made `notice` the same or worse.
**There is no host-side poll-strategy fix for `notice`.** The task brief's prior
is confirmed at the shipping operating point and refuted only in the direction
that does not help.

**Not explained: the transfer is sub-linear.** A uniformly-distributed final
sleep of `poll_us` should add `poll_us/2` to `notice` — +0.125 ms at 250 µs and

tested: (a) handing back ~5400 uncached DDR reads per frame speeds the fabric
and/or the observing read enough to partly cancel the granularity cost; (b)
`nanosleep`'s real wake distribution is not uniform against the `C_DONE` write.
Recorded as §6.3 rather than resolved — it does not change the direction of the
result, only its slope.

## 5. Verdict against spec §3 Stage A

Stage A asked: *"does host-side polling strategy change `notice`?"* — and framed
the branch as "if it moves, the ~0.40 ms really is DDR read-visibility; if it
does not, `notice` lives in the fabric."

**Answer: it does not move at any setting that could help, and 0.335 ms of the
0.558 ms is positively located in the fabric.** The dichotomy as written is
slightly too clean, so both halves, stated separately:

- **The DDR read-visibility inference is refuted as a *host-poll* proposition.**
  No poll strategy lowers `notice`; the shipping spin is already at the host
  floor (§4). Spec §3's optimistic branch — "the probe itself may indicate the
  cheapest fix" — does not obtain. There is no cheap host fix.
- **It is NOT refuted as a *write-visibility* proposition.** The 0.237 ms floor
  still contains the `C_DONE`-write-to-host-visible path, and a doorbell-delay
  probe cannot see it: the probe moves the *host's write*, not the *fabric's*.
  Poll rate bounds observation *quantisation* (≲1.5 µs), not write posting.

**§5 R1 obtains, in its main clause.** `notice` has a proven fabric-side
component and the ~0.40 ms cannot be closed from the host. The measured
fabric dead window (0.335 ms) is ~2.1× the 0.158 ms `S_SNAP` design figure, which
is R1's own phrasing — "`S_SNAP` running longer than its design figure, or
submit-observe latency".

**R1's contingency test sits right on the bar and does not resolve.** R1 says:
*if no fabric fix is sized ≥ 0.35 ms, the LW-bridge doorbell move becomes the
lever.* The proven fabric-side share is **0.335 ms**, 0.015 ms short of the bar,
with **P(K ≥ 0.35) ≈ 0.32–0.35** (window noise only / run-to-run folded in) and a
90 % CI of [0.275, 0.390] that **contains 0.35**. So:

- A Stage C fix that closes only the doorbell dead window is **more likely than
  not to fall short of 0.35 ms on its own** — call it ~2 chances in 3 — and would
  then need part of the 0.237 ms floor to clear R1's bar.
- But **this measurement does not trip R1's contingency.** "Almost certainly
  short" is not what the corpus says at the brief's window filter; roughly one in
  three is. If the LW-bridge branch is being taken *because* the fabric fix
  cannot reach 0.35 ms, that premise is not established here. (The looser
  `blocked ≥ 60 %` filter would put the probability at 0.08 and *would* read as
  "almost certainly short" — see §3's sensitivity table. The brief specified the
  tighter filter, and it is also the better fit.)
- If the 0.35 ms bar is genuinely the deciding criterion for an expensive
  compile, the cheapest way to settle it is more replicates, not a different
  analysis: §7.1 sizes what that would take.

**§5 R2 does not obtain.** R2 is "`notice` cannot yield 0.35 ms by any means".
The sweep does not support that: 0.335 ms is demonstrably recoverable *in
principle* — the fabric can be made to accept a doorbell it is currently
deaf to — and the 0.237 ms floor is unattributed rather than shown immovable.
R2's branch (ship §2b alone and re-fund Candidate A) is **not** triggered by this
measurement.

**Not claimed:** that any specific RTL change recovers 0.335 ms. The sweep
measures how long the fabric is deaf; it does not measure how much of that
deafness is removable. That is Stage C's problem and is not settled here.

## 7. Data-trust caveats — Stage A

1. **Sample depth, and what the CI does and does not cover.** Every primary
   heavy-band arm mean rests on **4–5 MFSEAM windows and 118–149 blocked frames**
   (5–7 windows / 146–197 blocked frames on the `≥ 60 %` sensitivity set); every
   exact-anchor mean on **1–2 windows and 28–60 blocked frames**. The
   exact-anchor table (1b) is a consistency check, not evidence on its own — a
   single-window arm has no internal spread. The knee's primary CI comes from
   bootstrapping windows within arms, so it captures window-to-window variation
   but **not** run-to-run variation; the unmodified replicates are the only
   handle on the latter — spread 0.073 / sd 0.036 (heavy band, n=3) and 0.122
   (exact anchor, n=4) against a 0.33 ms effect. §3 therefore prints a second CI
   with that sd folded in as a per-arm offset ([0.260, 0.410],
   `P(K ≥ 0.35) = 0.35`), and **that is the figure to quote in a decision**, not
   the window-only one. Three replicates is still thin: the folded CI is itself
   built on a 3-point sd. Neither CI should be read to two significant figures.
2. **Scene identity rests on a numeric gate only.** `cov_px == 213358` at
   `frame = 16.685–16.690 ms` in all thirteen logs, reproducing the W2 anchor
   exactly. **No screenshot was taken this phase.** W2's own §7.1 records that
   heavy-B has no screenshot pedigree at all. The numeric reproduction is of the
   W2 heavy-B *anchor value*, and it is worth being precise about what it is
   **not**: **heavy-B is not a plateau.** §1b measures it as a transient at most
   three windows (~90 frames, ~1.5 s) wide, with the 213,358 peak sampled on
   30–60 frames, bracketed by `cov_px ≈ 151,000` below and a collapse to
   105,000–141,000 above. Earlier write-ups (this document's own first draft
   included) described it as a plateau reproduced from Stage A; that framing is
   wrong and any downstream task that tries to hold heavy-B for a sustained
   capture will fail. Triangle count was never used to identify the scene.
3. **Arm-to-arm window membership is not perfectly matched, and the mismatch is
   conservative.** On the primary set the two heaviest arms are D=100 (mean
   `cov_px` 236,757) and D=300 (235,626), against ~229,600–231,300 for the other
   five — a 3.1 % spread. Within the unmodified runs `notice` rises weakly with
   scene weight (0.537 at `frame` 15–17 ms, 0.543 at 17–25; on the `≥ 60 %` set
   the gradient is steeper: 0.471 at 13–15, 0.543 at 15–17, 0.554 at 17–25), so a
   heavier membership biases that arm's `notice` **up**. Both heavy arms read
   *at or below* the fit — D=100 measured 0.449 against a fitted 0.455, D=300
   measured 0.220 against a fitted 0.237 — and D=300 read the lowest `notice` of
   any arm. The bias therefore works against the knee, not for it. Same argument
   for `poll_us=250` (membership `frame` 19.185 ms, still read inside the
   unmodified band).
4. **`period` is limiter-clamped and must not be read outside blocked windows.**
   With the shipping 60 fps cap, `period` pins near 16.69 ms on any frame the
   fabric finishes early, and in those windows `notice` is a 1–2-frame sample
   and swings wildly (single windows at 2.35, 4.70 ms). Every headline number in
   this document comes from `blocked ≥ 90 %` windows for that reason, with
   `blocked ≥ 60 %` reported as a sensitivity. The limiter sits inside `host` —
   `pub` is 0.00 on all unmodified windows — so it does not corrupt the probe's
   insertion point.
5. **The x-axis is measured `pub`, not requested `D`.** The probe overshoots by
   11 % (§2, self-check 1). A knee quoted against requested `D` would read
   ≈300 µs; against real inserted delay it is **335 µs**. The latter is the
   physically meaningful figure and is what §3 and §5 use.
6. **`period = host + block + pub` is exact per window; `period = frame + notice
   + pub` is a derived identity and is not.** The first holds to printing
   precision on **all 1,685 windows in all thirteen logs** (max residual
   0.010 ms, which is the 0.01 ms field width). The second substitutes the
   per-window `frame` for `block` and the per-blocked-frame mean `notice` for the
   per-window one, so it only closes as `blocked → 100 %`. Measured residuals
   `period − (frame + notice + pub)`, all over the seven sweep arms: on the exact
   anchor (93–100 % blocked) **−0.010 to +0.147 ms**, worst cases **+0.147**
   (D=200) and **+0.140** (D=300); arm-pooled on the primary `blocked ≥ 90 %`
   heavy band, **+0.008 to +0.203 ms**; on the `≥ 60 %` set, **+0.203 to
   +0.455 ms**. Single windows are worse — a 97 %-blocked anchor window (D=200,
   `n=1950`) reaches **+0.300**, and the worst 90 %-blocked window reaches
   **+0.93**. **The earlier claim that this reconciles "to ~0.05 ms" was wrong by
   3–6×.** The residual is comparable to the 0.237 ms floor, so the derived
   identity must not be used to cross-check or to back out any term at that
   scale; use `period = host + block + pub`, which is exact.
7. **Everything is one device, one bitstream, one session** (`.62`, 09:32–10:06
   on 2026-07-31, ambient conditions unrecorded). The four unmodified replicates
   are runs **1, 2, 9 and 13 of 13** (09:32, 09:35, 09:45, 10:02), so the session
   is bracketed at both ends: run 13 was added specifically to give the late poll
   arms an unmodified neighbour. Its exact-anchor `notice` (0.600) sits inside the
   earlier spread, so no session-level drift is visible at the anchor. A single
   session still cannot rule out a common-mode offset affecting all arms equally
   — but such an offset would shift the knee's *floor*, not the knee itself, since
   the knee is a difference between arms measured minutes apart.
8. **Run 13 is duration-anomalous and its heavy-band pool is unusable.** Its
   engine produced 9,630 frames against 2,790–3,480 elsewhere: the harness's
   per-second `ssh` sole-engine assertion ran slow, stretching the sampling loop
   to ~170 s of engine time. All integrity gates are clean (one handler start, no
   frame-counter reset, `suspect`/`incomplete`/`submit timeout` all 0, `to=0`) and
   the exact-anchor window is a valid like-for-like sample, but the heavy band
   pools later-game windows no other run reached, so its 0.490 band figure is
   reported and excluded rather than blended. *What would answer whether the slow
   `ssh` also perturbed the measurement:* re-run it and check the frame count
   matches the others.

### 8.3 Observed — why it is half the plan's estimate, and why that is a ceiling **[SIM]**

The plan sized Stage B at **−0.2125 ms** (real-cache `setup` 10,976 + `vfetch`
9,940 = 20,916 cyc). Task 1's re-measured baseline gave 10,976 + 9,846 = 20,822
cyc = 0.2115 ms on the stub bench. Both figures assume **every** triangle is
prefetchable.

A hierarchical probe over the frame (`w3probe.sv`, scratch, not committed) shows
why that assumption is false:

```
stream_heavy_f0: cmds=98  tri_cnt_min=2 tri_cnt_max=2 | swait_entries=196 seeded=196 rejected=0
                 | next_fast=98 next_serial=0 next_holdcyc=0
stream_quiet_f0: cmds=106 tri_cnt_min=2 tri_cnt_max=2 | swait_entries=212 seeded=160 rejected=52
                 | next_fast=80 next_serial=26 next_holdcyc=309
```

**Every TRILIST command carries exactly 2 triangles**, because the engine emits
one TRILIST per sprite quad (`raster_backend_mfgpu.cpp:2040`, `nt == 2`). The
prefetch is scoped to a single command by design — its arming guard is
`tri_idx + 1 < tri_count`, and crossing a command boundary would mean
prefetching the next *ring entry* and speculatively decoding its opcode.

**Inferred:** on a 2-triangle command stream, triangle 0 of each command is
necessarily serial, so **50 % of `setup + vfetch` is the design's arithmetic
ceiling** — and the implementation hits it exactly on heavy-B:

- 98 of 98 eligible `S_TRI_NEXT` transitions took the fast path
  (`next_serial = 0`, `next_holdcyc = 0`, zero rejects);
- `setup` after = 98 fast × 1 seed cycle + 98 serial × 56.0 cyc = **5,586** —
  the measured value **to the cycle**;
- quiet corroborates with a *different* split, 132 × 56 + 80 × 1 = **7,472**,
  also to the cycle. Quiet recovers less because 52 of its 212 triangles are
  rejected in `S_TRI_SWAIT` (degenerate / off-screen); a rejected triangle never
  enters `S_TRI_PIX`, so the prefetch never arms and 26 successors fall back to
  the serial path.

**The shortfall is in the plan's estimate, not in the implementation.** Any
downstream arithmetic that used −0.2125 ms is wrong and must be re-derived from
−0.104 ms — including the W3 funding decision and the 60 fps gap budget. That
re-derivation is §13.1.

## 10. The `notice` attribution counter, and the corrected identity

### 10.1 What was built **[SIM]**

maldita `96bfc95` + `73f7631`, engine `ee7523e` + `d09d50a`.

The fabric now publishes two intervals, in fabric clocks, as two 16-bit fields of
`C_CMDCOUNT.hi`, each `cyc >> 3` and saturating at `0xFFFF` (5.33 ms of range at
98.4375 MHz — ~13× the expected magnitude):

- **`snap`** = `S_WR_DONE` entry (where `C_DONE(N−1)` is written) → `S_POLL_SUBMIT`
  entry. The fabric's doorbell dead window.
- **`detect`** = `S_POLL_SUBMIT` entry → the `S_CHK_NEW` new-work branch for
  frame *N*.

The two abut with no gap by construction. Published via a new state
`S_WR_NOTICE = 6'd14` (the lowest unused code; **nothing renumbered**),
sequenced `S_WR_COVPX → S_WR_NOTICE → S_WR_DONE` so the pair lands **before** the
doorbell. `tb_perf_publish_order` was extended red→green, and a deliberate
inversion of the chain produces the intended failure line — the ordering clause
is live, not vacuous.

Engine side: `MFSUBMIT` gains `snap= detect= blk=`, accumulated **only over
blocked frames** (the same `g_seam_blocked` gate `MFSEAM` uses for `notice`), with
`blk` printed so the denominator is visible.

Rider on the same commit: `pv` (the prefetch sub-FSM state) on the debug bus at
`[8:6]`, with every pre-existing field left at its original offset
(`dbg_stuck[31:24]`, `rd_issued[23]`, `rd_reissue_cnt[22:15]`, `state[5:0]`).
Rationale: with the prefetch present, a dead f2h read during a walk now parks the
machine at `S_TRI_NEXT` with `rd_issued = 0` — the opposite signature from the
pre-prefetch `S_RD_WAIT` / `rd_issued = 1` one, and indistinguishable from a
benign `PV_HOLD` without the field.

Cost: rasterizer buckets identical, `total`/`other` +4 cyc (the extra publish
write). `exact_bad = 0`. PR suite 54 pass.

### 10.2 The identity was wrong in three places, and is now corrected **[DERIVED]**

The counter shipped with a false claim attached to it, in the RTL comment, the
engine comment and the task report:
`notice − (snap + detect) = ε`, where `ε` is the host's own `C_DONE` observation
latency — i.e. the counter would read the 0.237 ms floor directly.

**The correct identity is `notice = snap + detect − pub`.** `ε` cancels. Two
independent derivations agree, and agree on the mechanism: the host spends `ε`
*inside* the snap tail (`t2 = t0 + ε + pub` on a blocked frame), so the fabric
eats it off the front of `snap` and re-exposes it exactly once, after `C_DONE(N)`.
It is not left over by the subtraction.

Three consequences, all now written into the RTL, the engine and the report:

1. **The counter cannot settle `ε`.** Rearranged, `(t3 − t2) + ε = snap − pub`:
   one equation, two unknowns. The pair pins the *sum* of the exposed tail and
   the host's observation latency, never the split. Separating them needs a host
   timestamp referenced to the fabric clock, which no instrument in this phase
   provides. The old comment implicitly claimed `ε` had been measured; it has
   not been, and by this algebra it cannot be from this instrument.
2. **Published `snap` over-states the exposed fabric dead window by `(ε + pub)`.**
   Using Stage A's floor as `ε` and its measured `pub` ≈ 0.00:
   exposed ≈ `snap − ε − pub` ≈ 0.335 − 0.237 = **0.098 ms** — **[DERIVED]**
   from the Stage A fit, not measured, and not independent of it.
3. **The identity holds on blocked frames only.** On a host-late frame the
   barrier returned early on an already-visible `C_DONE`, so `t2 − t0 > ε + pub`,
   `detect` absorbs the entire overrun, and `snap + detect − pub` over-states
   `notice` by all of it. This is why the accumulators are gated on
   `g_seam_blocked`, and it is exactly the regime §10.3 landed in.

Two known biases, both one-directional (they make `snap` read **high**), both
documented rather than fixed:

- `snap` arms at `S_WR_DONE` **entry**, not at the `C_DONE` write being accepted,
  so it includes the `S_WR_WAIT` bus-accept cycles (~4 in sim; potentially more
  on device under arbiter contention).
- A ±1-cycle undercount on each interval, so the *sum* is short of the true
  window by 2 cycles — ~20 ns, 4 × 10⁻⁵ of the signal. Correcting it would mean
  touching verified counting logic, so it is stated and left.

**Falsifiable prediction for the first device read**, from Stage A's
decomposition: `snap ≈ 0.335`, `detect ≈ 0.230`, on heavy-B at
`blocked ≈ 100 %`.

## 14. The frame-1 wedge investigation

**Symptom**, reported by the user after the W3 deploy: black video, `C_SUBMIT`
climbing, `C_DONE = 0`, `frame_hi = 0`, 3,841 dropped frames; audio unaffected.

**Observed.** The scanout frame counter at `0x3BFB0018` was advancing at ~60 Hz
throughout, so the display path was healthy and only the fabric was wedged.

**A/B, by core reload without reboot:** `a723aa5` 2/2 clean, `73f7631` 2/2 clean.
Wedges were **2 of 4 loads across both bitstreams**.

**Verdict: the documented pre-existing intermittent frame-1 wedge.** Not a W3
regression, and not deterministic on reload.

**Refuted along the way**, stated because it was the first hypothesis: that the
prefetch, acting as a second DDR3 requester, caused it. The wedged fabric had
`frame_hi = 0`, so it never reached a TRILIST walk and `pv` never ran.

**Open, pre-existing, and worth its own work.** The `S_RD_WAIT` reissue watchdog
(`RW_WD_MAX` ≈ 42.6 ms) exists to recover a lost first f2h read and should
self-heal this in ~43 ms. It **did not**, over thousands of frames. The watchdog
does not cover the case that actually occurs at core-load bring-up.
*What would answer it:* read the debug bus during a live wedge — `state`,
`rd_issued`, `rd_reissue_cnt`, and now `pv` — and establish whether the machine
is parked in `S_RD_WAIT` with `rd_issued = 1` (inside the watchdog's scope) or
somewhere the watchdog cannot see. The `pv` field was added in this phase
specifically so that post-mortem is possible.

### 14.1 Root cause found and fixed — maldita PR #23, and what it did *not* fix

**The mechanism.** `f2sdram_safe_terminator` passed `waitrequest` straight
through while `terminating`. An Avalon-MM command asserted with `waitrequest` low
was therefore **accepted** by the master but never reached the port — so the
master waits forever for a `readdatavalid` nobody will ever produce. One-line
fix: `waitrequest_slave = waitrequest_master | (terminating & ~read_terminating)`.

It explains the frame-1 signature exactly. The blitter polls `C_SUBMIT`
immediately out of reset, and `rst_req_sync` is double-registered onto `ram1_clk`,
so the terminator stays locked ~3 cycles *after* the fabric leaves reset. It is
gated on `init_reset_deasserted`, which is why first boot never shows it and
**every subsequent `load_core` can**.

Merged into `perf/phase4-w3` as `4625fdc`. **Zero file overlap with any W3
